Welcome to your 2025 farm report. This report summarizes the on-farm nitrogen experiment conducted in your field, where cover crop strips (treatment) were compared against no-cover-crop controls.

Take-home message: Across all four nitrogen indicators (NBI, Chlorophyll, Biomass %N, and CSNT) corn plants in the cover crop strips showed better nitrogen status than those in the control strips. Leaf readings were greener and more nitrogen-sufficient, plant tissue had noticeably more nitrogen, and end-of-season stalk nitrate revealed a clear difference between treatments: control stalks were well below the sufficiency threshold, while cover crop stalks were within the optimal range. Keep in mind that the tissue and stalk results come from very few samples, so they are early signals rather than proof. Your soil health score is solid overall, but building biological activity through continued cover cropping is the clearest path to unlocking even more nitrogen from the soil over time.

🌿 Nitrogen Balance Index (NBI)

The Nitrogen Balance Index (NBI) is measured with the Dualex sensor and reflects the ratio of chlorophyll to flavonoids in the leaf. A higher NBI indicates better nitrogen status, the plant has enough N to build chlorophyll without accumulating excess flavonoids as a stress response. We took two readings per plant on 175 plants (122 in the cover crop strips, 53 in the control), averaged them, and compared the two groups with a two-sample t-test. The difference was statistically significant, meaning it is unlikely to be explained by random variation between plants.

🍃 Chlorophyll (Chl)

🧪 C:N in Corn Biomass

Corn biomass was collected and sent for laboratory C:N analysis. This measures the actual nitrogen concentration in the plant tissue, a direct indicator of how much N the plant was able to take up. Note: We only collected a few biomass samples (3 control, 7 treatment), which is too few for a reliable statistical test. Instead, we report an “effect size” (Cohen’s d). It answers a simple question: is the gap between the cover crop and control strips bigger than the normal differences you see from one sample to the next within the same strip? A value near 0 means the two strips look about the same. A value above 0.8 means the gap clearly stands out from that normal variation. With so few samples, treat it as a hint rather than proof.

🌽 Cornstalk Nitrate Test (CSNT)

The Cornstalk Nitrate Test (CSNT) measures nitrate concentration in the lower cornstalk at the end of the season. It is a post-harvest diagnostic tool: values below 250 ppm indicate insufficient nitrogen, while the optimal range is between 750 and 2000 ppm. Because only one sample per treatment was collected, no statistical comparison is possible, this is a field-level observation.

🌱 Soil Health Assessment

Soil health was assessed using the Cornell Soil Health Test (sampled June 11, 2025, Field 3). The overall quality score was 69/100 (High). Chemical indicators are strong: pH (6.3), phosphorus and potassium all scored 98 or higher. On the physical side, water holding capacity (87) and surface hardness (75) came back well, while aggregate stability (55) and subsurface hardness (59) are in the Medium range. The area to watch is biological activity: soil respiration scored 16 (Very Low), and active carbon and soil protein both scored 34 (Low), which means the microbial community that breaks down organic matter and cycles nutrients isn’t as active as it could be. Cover cropping works on exactly this: it feeds soil microbes and adds carbon, which helps build biological activity over time.

📝 Conclusions

🌿 Leaf Nitrogen Status (NBI)
Cover crop strips showed a 13.4% higher NBI compared to the control (76 vs 67). Corn plants in the cover crop strips had better nitrogen status mid-season, and the difference was statistically significant.
🍃 Chlorophyll Index (Chl)
Chlorophyll values were also higher in cover crop strips (+6.9%; 35.4 vs 33.1), consistent with the NBI result. Higher chlorophyll reflects greener, more nitrogen-sufficient leaves.
🧪 Biomass Nitrogen (%N)
Laboratory analysis of corn biomass showed treatment plants had +17.6% more nitrogen in their tissue on average (2.286% vs 1.944%). The effect size (Cohen's d = 0.57) is moderate, but with only 3 control and 7 treatment samples, and values that overlap between the two groups, this difference could be due to chance.
🌽 End-of-Season CSNT
The control stalk sample came in at 79 ppm, below the 250 ppm sufficiency threshold, while the cover crop sample came in at 938 ppm, within the optimal range (750–2,000 ppm). This is a large gap and it points in the same direction as the leaf readings, but it comes from a single sample per treatment, so it cannot be tested statistically and should be confirmed with more samples next season.
🌱 Soil Health
Your overall soil health score of 69/100 (High) reflects good water holding capacity and nutrient levels, with low biological activity: soil respiration (score 16), active carbon (score 34) and soil protein (score 34). Cover cropping adds organic inputs that feed soil microbes, so continuing it is a reasonable way to work on these indicators over time.
